# Design principles

Kairos is built from CBT-for-ADHD techniques and executive-function research, not from a to-do-list template. Every feature maps to a specific friction.

## 1. Time must be visible

ADHD brains don't *feel* time pass (time blindness). Numbers changing on a clock don't help; shapes shrinking do.

- The timer is a disc whose coloured wedge shrinks — a digital Time Timer. Digits are secondary and placed low.
- The **Day bar** shows the waking day as a bar with a dot at "now".
- Durations are converted to anchors ("about one episode") in the MCP tools.
- **Time-guess calibration** surfaces the personal multiplier between guess and reality — a classic CBT exercise — phrased as information, never as a failing.

## 2. Starting beats finishing

Task paralysis is the real enemy. Once the first step is visible and tiny, the body moves.

- The biggest button on the screen is **Just Start (5 mins)**.
- **Magic Breakdown** always makes step one trivially easy ("Open the textbook — just open it").
- Only the **next step** is shown; the rest hide behind a toggle.
- Completion offers *Keep going (+5)* as the primary action — momentum is cheaper than restarting.
- Partway always counts: stopping early is "Done for now", never "abandoned".

## 3. Shame is not a strategy

Guilt lowers follow-through. Most productivity apps are built out of it.

- **No streaks.** Insights only count things that go up.
- **No red.** The palette has no alert colour; gentle notices use a warm clay.
- **No overdue.** Tasks have no due dates to miss. **Fresh Start** archives everything open without judgement.
- **The companion cannot die.** It grows from focus and *rests* when you're away. Mood states are cozy / awake / resting — nothing negative exists in the code.
- **Daily anchors keep no history.** Yesterday is literally not stored.
- Copy is reviewed against a banned list: *should have, again, overdue, missed, failed, lazy, simple, just*.

## 4. Externalise working memory

Working memory is the bottleneck. Get it out of the head.

- **Parking lot**: the CBT "distractibility delay" made concrete — write it, hit Enter, return to the task. The input blurs on purpose so the bar doesn't become the new focus.
- **Brain dump**, **timestamped notes**, **page notes**, **anchors** all do the same job in different contexts.
- Parked thoughts resurface *after* focus, collapsed by default.

## 4b. Close the loop every 90 seconds

Passive input (reading, watching) is where ADHD attention leaks. The fix is a short, physical feedback loop.

- **Kinetic Reading**: text is cut into ~110-word sections. Everything below is blurred. The next section unblurs only when the reader types or drags the key word from the section they just read. Hands move, a micro-reward lands, attention re-engages — roughly every 60–90 seconds.
- The puzzle is forgiving on purpose: case and one-letter typos are accepted; after two misses it offers the answer; "skip" is always one click. A speedbump that becomes a wall is just shame with extra steps.
- **Lecture mode** does the same job for video: pause at a chunk boundary, ask for one line.
- The **YouTube extension** carries the same loop onto youtube.com itself: every ten watched minutes the video pauses until you summarise the section in a sentence, and a parking-lot button sits on the page so a stray thought doesn't become a tab switch.

## 4c. Make the reward uncertain

Dopamine responds to *anticipated, uncertain* reward more than to the reward itself.

- **Loot-drop breaks**: finishing a sprint offers "Claim reward", which rolls a 3-minute breather, a 7-minute break, a movement challenge, an item from your own dopamine menu, or (rarely) a short embedded clip. Rarity labels make the roll legible.
- Every outcome is a real, bounded break. Clips end on their own with related videos suppressed; there is no infinite surface anywhere in the drop.
- You can always skip the drop and keep going, and the whole mechanic has an off switch.

## 4d. Manufacture the deadline

ADHD brains find urgency late. Rather than scold, we fake a little of it, gently.

- **Final-stretch audio**: the lo-fi soundscape runs at 72 BPM; across the last three minutes it rises to 86, the filter opens, hats get busier and a bassline fades in. Noise soundscapes get a slow tremolo at the same rising tempo.
- It is a felt cue, never an alarm — no voice, no beep, no red. Off by one toggle.

## 4e. Make completion physical

Ticking a box is too cheap for a brain that needs a felt payoff.

- **Swipe-to-complete**: finishing a task takes a full drag across the row, then a deep sub-bass thud and a brief particle burst. The gesture is big enough that it's a small ritual, not an accident; the checkbox triggers the same celebration so keyboard users aren't short-changed.
- Reduced motion keeps the thud and drops the burst; sound off keeps the burst and drops the thud. Both never, ever vibrate the device or flash the screen.

## 4f. Notice stuck before it becomes quitting

Frustration with ADHD tends to be silent: ten minutes staring at the same paragraph, or clicking around at random, then the tab closes.

- **Stuck detection** watches for exactly those two signatures — no interaction on the same chunk/page/step for 10 minutes, or nine aimless clicks in four seconds — and fires at most once per context.
- The response is a small card in the corner, not a modal: *"Stuck? Let me summarise this, give you an analogy, or cut it into smaller pieces."* For a task it offers to shrink the current step. "I'm fine" dismisses it and it stays quiet for that context.
- The wording is about the material being hard, never about the person being slow.

## 4g. Borrow other people's presence, not their attention

Body doubling — working alongside someone — is one of the best-documented ADHD aids, but video calls and chat are their own distraction.

- **Focus pods** match 2–4 people into one 25-minute countdown. No camera, no mic, no chat, no names beyond a nickname you can change. The whole social surface is a row of coloured dots and "3 others are focusing right now".
- Joining a pod starts your timer to the pod's clock; leaving never stops a block in progress. Your sprint is yours.

## 4h. Match the sound to the task, not the mood

Under-stimulation makes boring work impossible; over-stimulation makes hard work impossible. Music is the cheapest dial we have.

- The **Cognitive audio engine** shifts Spotify by *mental load*: a low-load task (filing, copying, inbox) gets a high-energy playlist to supply the missing stimulation; a high-load task (proofs, dense reading) gets ambient, scores or brown noise so nothing competes.
- Auto mode reads the task's energy tag, so the choice was made when the task was captured, not when willpower is lowest. A two-way override (Brain-dead · Deep work) is always one click away.
- The switch dips the volume rather than cutting — a jolt is exactly what we're trying to avoid.

## 4i. Take away the finish line when it hurts

For many ADHD brains a countdown is a threat: watching time run out produces anxiety, and stopping early feels like failing.

- **Open flow** counts *up* from zero with no target. Zen view hides the digits behind a slowly breathing circle whose colour drifts too slowly to notice moving.
- Every ten minutes a small, silent burst marks the milestone — a reward that arrives without asking anything of you.
- There is no Stop. There is **Capture session**, and whatever was captured counts: thirty seconds is "you started".

## 4j. Give the hands the controls

Listening is passive; steering is not. **Listen** puts read-aloud on the keyboard — space, arrows, brackets — and every keypress flashes its keycap, so pacing becomes something you physically do.

## 4k. Fast and free beats clever

Waiting on a network is a gap where attention leaks. Speedbumps, breakdowns and summaries run on the device in milliseconds; the cloud is an explicit opt-in for the leftovers, not the default path.

## 5. Reduce decisions

Deciding is a drain. Pre-decide where possible.

- **Dopamine menu**: breaks chosen in advance, in three sizes, with a dice.
- Preset block lengths (5 / 10 / 15 / 25) rather than a free field.
- Energy tags let a tired brain filter to "low energy" tasks instead of scanning everything.

## 6. Low stimulation by default

- **Tactile minimalism.** Primary buttons have a bottom lip that recedes when pressed — objects, not flat colour. Cards sit on warm parchment (`#F7F5F0`) as bone (`#EBE9E1`) surfaces, separated by soft shadow rather than harsh borders.
- **Spatial anchoring.** Four persistently visible destinations (focus, tasks, study, care). Settings lives in a quiet corner, never a hamburger. Places don't move.
- **Progressive disclosure.** Only the current step is sharp and centred; later steps exist as a blurred whisper until their turn.
- **Cognitive calm palette.** Parchment canvas, bone cards, sage (`#84A59D`) for action, deep slate (`#2C363F`) for type, terracotta (`#E27D60`) for the rare notice. No pure white, no pure black, no neon, no alarm red.
- Outfit, entirely lowercase for the product name and display type — geometric, approachable, no aggressive authority.
- Soft two-note chime, never an alarm. Soundscapes are opt-in and synthesised quietly.
- Motion is small, and fully disabled by a setting or `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- In focus mode the header disappears, the nav dims, and only timer + current step + parking lot remain.

## 7. Private by default

- Everything lives in the browser. No account, no analytics, no tracking.
- PDFs render locally. Videos use the privacy-enhanced YouTube embed.
- Export is a JSON file the user owns; import restores it.
- The MCP server is stateless and never sees local data.
- Focus pods transmit a random id, a nickname and a focusing/idle flag, expiring after a minute. Spotify tokens never leave the browser (PKCE, no server). The extension makes no network requests at all.

## Accessibility baseline

- All controls are real buttons/inputs with labels; radio groups and switches use ARIA roles.
- Timer announces at minute boundaries via a polite live region, not every second.
- Visible, non-aggressive focus ring; keyboard hotkeys (`/`, `Esc`) for the parking lot.
- Text size, letter/word spacing, and motion are user-adjustable.
- Colour contrast meets WCAG AA for text in both themes.
